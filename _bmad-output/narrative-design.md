## Story Foundation

### Narrative Premise

A band of spirit warriors has been chosen to purge the corruption spreading through their tribe's ancestral territories. Run after run, they venture out — down the western road toward the Plains, down the eastern road toward the Forest — to face twisted spirits and drive the darkness back. Every territory purified flows strength back to the hub village: the campfire burns brighter, the sanctuary grows more alive. But the corruption is patient. The roads grow darker the longer the warriors wait.

### Core Themes

**1. Bonds Forged in Struggle**
Warriors grow stronger together — not just mechanically, but spiritually. The Spirit Bond system is the story made tangible: the bonds you form in the heat of battle are real, earned through shared danger. No warrior purifies the world alone.

**2. Healing a Wounded World**
The goal is not destruction — it is purification. The ancestral spirits were once sacred guardians; the corruption twisted them. Warriors do not kill enemies, they cleanse them. The world was whole once, and can be again — one territory at a time.

**3. Knowledge Through Adversity**
The corruption is not random. It has logic, pattern, depth. The more warriors face it, the more they understand it — its tells, its rhythms, its roots. Mastery is not just skill; it is earned understanding of a cursed world. Veterans read what newcomers cannot yet see.

---

### Tone and Atmosphere

**Tone:** Warm · Serious · Epic · Hopeful

**Atmosphere:** The hub village radiates safety and earned warmth — firelit, alive, a sanctuary that pulses with the strength the warriors bring home. The dungeon is the opposite: tense, darkening, serious. Not horror — the mood is urgency and purpose. Combat is epic and high-frequency. The emotional register is always hopeful: even in the darkest level, the warriors are *together*, and they are pushing back.

**Emotional arc per session:** Safe → Purposeful → Pressured → Triumphant → Home

---

### Story Structure

**Episodic with Overarching World Arc**

Each run is a self-contained episode — a complete story with a beginning, a trail, and a reckoning. Across many runs, the world accumulates: corruption retreats, the hub brightens, new territories open. There is no final resolution — the cycle is the covenant. Warriors are not saving the world once; they are keeping it, run after run, generation after generation.

### Act Breakdown

**Per-Run Episode — The Trail:**

| Beat | What happens | Emotional feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Departure** | Warriors leave the warmth of the hub, walking the road toward the corrupted territory | Purposeful, serious |
| **The Trail (Level 1)** | First signs of corruption — scattered, readable, traceable. Enemy behavior erratic but readable. Environment shows early decay | Cautious, focused |
| **Deeper (Level 2)** | Corruption intensifies. Enemies grow more frenzied, environment more consumed. The trail narrows. Spirit Bonds form under pressure | Pressure mounts, bonds tested |
| **The Root (Level 3)** | The source is close. The environment is fully corrupted. Enemies are desperate. The warriors can feel the boss ahead | Urgent, epic |
| **The Reckoning (Boss)** | The dominant corrupted spirit — the root of this territory's infection. Every mechanic from the levels converges. The bonds hold or break here | Climactic, earned |
| **The Return** | Territory purified. Warriors carry the cleansing back to the hub. The campfire burns a little brighter | Hopeful, warm |

**World Arc — The Covenant:**

The cycle has no end — only seasons. Corruption retreats when warriors push, returns when they rest. The hub is a living record of their effort: brighter after victories, dimmer in neglect. New roads open as territories are purified. The world does not remember individual warriors, but the campfire does.

---

### Beat Placement

**Act 1 — Into the Dark (Departure through First Bond)**
Warriors leave safety, enter the corrupted world, read the first signs of the trail, and form their first bond. Tone: purposeful and cautious. The bond moment is the first exhale.

**Act 2 — The Trail Narrows (Levels 2–3, Bonds 2–3, Near-Wipe)**
Escalation. Corruption thickens. Bonds accumulate. The near-wipe is the darkest possible moment — the vigil of spirit forms surrounding the last fighter. Tone: pressured, epic, bonds tested.

**Act 3 — The Reckoning and Return (Boss, Purification, Return)**
The root confronted. Everything the run has built toward. Followed by the return home — warmth earned, not given. World arc beats register here: the campfire reacts, the road remembers.

## Pacing and Flow

### Narrative Tempo

Fast-paced combat with deliberate, player-controlled breathing room. The pace is never forced — after each level, the team rests at the bond moment and chooses when to advance. No level auto-loads. The urgency is self-imposed.

### Tension Curve

Escalating waves. Each level sits higher than the last on the tension scale, with brief decompression at each bond moment. The boss is the peak — and it should feel earned because of the three decompression beats that preceded it.

```
Tension
  │                                        ████ Boss
  │                              ███ L3   █
  │                     ██ L2   █    Bond█
  │            █ L1    █   Bond █
  │           █   Bond █
  │          █
  └──────────────────────────────────────── Time
    Hub  Depart  L1  Bond  L2  Bond  L3  Bond  Boss  Return
```

### Story Density

Very light. All narrative is environmental and VFX-driven — zero text-heavy moments during play. The world shows its state; players read it. The only direct communication is the Spirit Bond description on the controller, which is personal and brief.

### Key Moments

**Highest tension:** The Boss arena — maximum corruption, all bonds active, everything at stake.
**Most emotionally complex:** The Vigil — the last warrior surrounded by the spirit forms of fallen companions.
**Most hopeful:** The Return — campfire brightening, road slightly cleaner, the world holding the memory.

## Characters

### Protagonists — The Warriors

Ten warriors, ten tribes, one shared purpose. Each warrior is an emissary sent from their homeland to the border campsite — the threshold where clean and cursed land collide. They come from different traditions, communicate with different spirits, and carry different powers. At the hub, those differences become the team's strength.

**Motivation:** Keep the world safe. The hub is not a home — it is a post. Warriors do not choose to be here; they are sent. But they fight because what they protect is real.

**Personality:** Shaped by the player. Each class is one archetype, one tradition — the player gives them a name, a voice, a soul.

### Antagonist — The Corruption

**Description:** A parasitic dark force seeping through the earth, twisting the ancestral spirits it touches into hostile, broken versions of themselves. It is not sentient. It does not plan. But it *seeks* — drawn to living things, feeding on nature and animals, spreading through the land like an infection through soil.

**Origin:** The Corruption was not born — it was made. An ancient tribe, long since wiped out, pursued dark power beyond what the spirit world could bear. Their ritual cursed the land itself. The seed of that corruption runs too deep in the earth to be fully removed. Decades of purification can silence it, but it will always return.

**Behavior:** The Corruption spreads toward life. Corrupted spirits do not act with intelligence — they act with hunger. They are the ancestral guardians of their territory, broken and weaponized by the dark force inside them.

**What warriors are doing:** Purifying, not destroying. The living spirit beneath the corruption can be freed. Warriors cleanse the wound so what was sacred can rest again.

**Mythological name:** Known across all tribes simply as *the old curse* — the legacy of the power-hungry tribe that broke the land. No secret name. No mystery about its origin. Every tribe child knows the story: hubris left a scar, and the world bleeds.

---

### Supporting Characters

#### The Keeper
**Role:** Campfire guardian — practical, unchanging, eternal.
**Personality:** Steady. Neither warm nor cold. The campfire's steward has seen many warriors come and go. They do not mourn or celebrate; they tend.
**Function:** Information hub — guides, tutorials, enemy knowledge. The Keeper knows what lives in the corrupted territories and tells warriors what to expect.
**Key moment:** The Keeper is always at the fire. Before the first run and after the last. Unchanged.

#### The Vendor
**Role:** Cosmetics merchant.
**Personality:** Idle, present, brightens with the campsite as the campfire grows and light returns to the hub.
**Function:** Spirit Essence exchange — skins, cosmetic progression.
**Key moment:** Visibly more at ease as the camp's light grows. Not scripted — ambient.

#### The Campsite Gate
**Role:** Structural POI — the threshold from the clean continent.
**Function:** Class switch point. Players re-commit to their tribe's tradition here before each run.
**Narrative meaning:** The gate faces the clean world. It is a reminder of what the warriors are protecting and what they would return to.

## Character Arcs

### The Warrior Arc (per class)

**Starting state:** A warrior arrives from their tribe, carrying their spirit tradition into unfamiliar territory alongside strangers.

**Transformation:** Expressed through mastery — not story. The more a warrior uses their abilities, the deeper their bond with their spirit becomes. Ability variants, cosmetic milestones, and mastery markers show progression. Theme: *Knowledge Through Adversity.*

**Ending state:** A warrior who has run many times understands the corruption — its patterns, its roots, its desperation near the boss. That understanding is the arc.

### The Keeper Arc

**Flat arc.** The Keeper does not change — they are the constant. In a world that cycles between corruption and purification, the Keeper is the fixed point. Their presence is what makes the hub feel like a place worth returning to.

### The Hub Arc (collective)

**Starting state:** A dim campsite at the border — functional, sparse, the light just holding.

**Transformation:** With each run completed, each territory purified, the hub brightens. NPCs shift from subdued to alive. The campfire grows. The roads to the biomes grow cleaner.

**Ending state:** There is no ending — only the ongoing covenant. The hub will always need tending. But a bright hub is proof that the warriors held.

## World Building

### World Overview

**Setting:** A tribal fantasy world with no single name — called simply "the land" or "the world" by those who live in it. The world is inhabited by scattered tribal peoples, from ancient and deep-rooted villages to more developed settlements connected by trade routes. No central authority governs them.

**World Type:** Tribal spirit fantasy. Magic is the relationship between people and the ancestral spirits of the natural world — animals, plants, weather, terrain. Each tribe communes with different spirits, which shapes their traditions, their skills, and their warriors.

**World Rules:**
- Spirit power is real and personal — it must be cultivated through tradition and relationship, not wielded by anyone
- The Corruption is a physical force: it lives in the earth, spreads toward life, and can be purified but never permanently removed
- The corrupted spirits were once sacred guardians — purification frees what's underneath, it doesn't destroy it
- Many campsites exist across the world, each a frontier outpost on the border between clean and corrupted land

**Atmosphere:** Earthy, warm, inhabited. The world feels old and alive — ancient forests, open plains, firelit villages. The presence of corruption is visible but not overwhelming in the clean lands; at the frontier, the contrast is stark.

**Unique Elements:** The tribes are not unified by religion, government, or race — only by the shared fact of the curse and the shared language of trade. The hub campsite is the one place in the world where multiple tribes meet in significant numbers. It is the largest gathering most warriors will ever attend.

---

### History and Backstory

**Timeline:**

The curse is a few generations old — old enough that no living person remembers a world without it, but recent enough that it feels like history rather than myth. The story is known by every tribe child: an unnamed tribe, somewhere in the corrupted territory, pursued dark power through ritual. Their ritual went wrong. The land they stood on was cursed to the root.

That tribe no longer exists. What remains is the wound they made.

**Major Events:**

- **The Cursing** — The nameless tribe's ritual corrupted their land. The tribe was destroyed — by the curse, by neighboring tribes, or by both; the stories differ. Their name is not spoken; to name them is considered an invitation.
- **The Spread** — The corruption did not stay contained. Over generations it has bled outward, reaching new territories, corrupting new ancestral spirits. The frontier moves.
- **The First Campsites** — As the corruption spread, tribes near the frontier established border campsites — thresholds where warriors could gather, rest, and push back. No single tribe founded this tradition; it emerged independently across the world as necessity.
- **The Ongoing Campaign** — There is no end to this. Warriors have been purifying territories for generations. Each run pushes the corruption back. Each generation picks up the duty from the last.

**Legends and Myths:**

The nameless tribe is the cautionary myth of the world. Every tribe teaches it differently — some say the tribe sought to commune with spirits beyond their bloodline, others say they tried to harness the corruption itself as a weapon. The lesson is always the same: hubris breaks the world, and the living pay for it.

The campfire is believed by some tribes to be a spirit in its own right — not an individual spirit, but the collective warmth of every warrior who has ever returned from a run. Whether true or not, it burns differently at the hub than anywhere else.

**Hidden Secrets:**

None — the world does not hide its wound. Every tribe knows the curse exists, knows its rough origin, knows it cannot be fully healed. The honesty of the world is part of its character: the warriors are not discovering a truth, they are living one.

---

### Factions — The Tribes

No formal alliance exists. The tribes are connected by trade and by shared knowledge of the curse, but they govern themselves independently. Some tribes are warmer toward each other — old trade partnerships, intermarriage, overlapping spirit traditions. Others are cooler — distant, self-sufficient, strangers at the campsite.

The hub is their most significant point of contact. Warriors who fight together may return home with stories of other traditions, other ways of being. Trade sometimes follows.

### Key Locations

#### The Hub Campsite *(name TBD)*
**Description:** A border outpost at the threshold between clean and corrupted land. Sacred campfire at its center. Handbuilt structures — the Keeper's station, the Vendor's post, the class-switch gate facing the clean lands. Roads leading west and east into corrupted territories.
**Narrative significance:** The only place in the world where multiple tribes meet in numbers. The campfire is the hub's heart — its size and brightness reflects the strength of the warriors who tend it.
**Atmosphere:** Warm, firelit, purposeful. Not cosy by accident — cosy because warriors made it so.
**Inhabitants:** The Keeper, the Vendor, visiting warriors between runs.

#### The Grassland Plains *(name TBD)*
**Description:** Open corrupted territory to the west. Long sightlines, golden-turned-dim grass, herd-animal and plains spirits twisted by the curse.
**Narrative significance:** The first territory most warriors run. Where the trail begins. Corruption is visible but not yet overwhelming — a readable introduction to what the curse does to a living place.
**Atmosphere:** Open, exposed, faintly wrong. Soul cracks in the earth. Eyes in the grass.

#### The Ancient Forest *(name TBD)*
**Description:** Dense corrupted territory to the east. Canopied, dark, predator and tree spirits consumed by the curse. Tight corridors, ambush country.
**Narrative significance:** Harder, older, deeper-corrupted than the Plains. The forest feels like it has been sick longer.
**Atmosphere:** Enclosed, shadow-heavy, unsettling. Soul-cracked bark. Void-eyed predators between the trees.

#### The Clean Lands *(name TBD)*
**Description:** The world beyond the gate — what the warriors come from and what they are protecting. Scattered tribal villages, trade routes, ancient settlements and more developed ones.
**Narrative significance:** Players never see this in gameplay, but it exists behind the gate. It is the answer to "why are we here." The campfire's warmth is a small echo of it.
**Atmosphere:** Alive, diverse, ordinary. The world as it should be.

## Dialogue Framework

### Dialogue Style

**Overall Voice:** Warm and direct. The world speaks plainly — no flowery prose, no overwrought drama. Every line earns its place. The writing is confident enough to be brief.

**Style Elements:**

| Aspect | Value |
|---|---|
| Formality | Warm-casual to mythic, depending on speaker |
| Period | Not modern — timeless, tribal register |
| Verbosity | Concise — short lines preferred |
| Humor | Occasional, situational, never forced |
| Profanity | None |

---

### Character Voice Distinctions

#### The Keeper — Dry and Steady
Plain language. No ceremony. Seen everything, says only what's needed. Not trying to be funny, but occasionally is through sheer understatement.

> *"East road is deep today. Watch the treeline."*
> *"Third run this evening. The corruption is still there."*
> *"The Forest boss has three phases. You'll find out."*

#### The Vendor — Warm and Chatty
The most human voice in the hub. Friendly, occasionally enthusiastic in a way that feels slightly at odds with the frontier setting. Brightens with the campsite.

> *"New skin just in — the spirits worked very hard on the color."*
> *"You're back. Good. The campfire was getting restless."*

#### Spirit Bond Descriptions — Direct, Personal, Occasionally Funny
Appears on the bonded players' controllers. Short. Addressed to the player personally. The bond mechanics have inherent awkwardness — the writing can lean into that warmth.

> *"You and [Player] are now bound by fate. If they fall, you fall. Maybe warn them."*
> *"A proximity bond links you to [Player]. Stay close. Or don't — see what happens."*
> *"You and [Player] share speed now. You also share consequences. Good luck."*

#### The Territory Spirit Voice — Ancient and Calm
Disembodied. Not a character who lives in the hub — something older, something that belongs to the territory itself. Brief, measured, neither warm nor cold. Speaks only at the two highest-stakes moments: boss defeated and run failure. Biome-specific variations.

**Boss Defeated:**
> *"The forest curse has been weakened. Rest now, champions."*
> *"The plains are quieter tonight. You did this."*

**Run Failure:**
> *"Tonight, the forest was worse than we anticipated... Maybe next time."*
> *"The plains held their ground. They will not always."*

#### Class Lore Introductions — Brief and Mythic
One or two sentences. Third-person. Reads like an inscription, not a bio.

## Environmental Storytelling

### Visual Storytelling

**The Corruption Trail — Level Progression:**
The environment is the story's most consistent narrator. Across the four beats of a run, the world visually tracks the warriors' journey into the root:
- **Level 1:** Mostly clean — scattered soul-cracked elements hint at what's ahead. Green and gold still dominate.
- **Level 2:** Half-consumed — corrupted and clean elements in tension. The trail is narrowing.
- **Level 3:** Nearly fully corrupted — clean elements are rare and feel precious. The boss is close.
- **Boss arena:** Maximum corruption. Every surface soul-cracked, every color wrong. The world at its sickest.

**Soul Cracks — The Universal Corruption Marker:**
Corrupted enemies and environmental elements show dark void fractures across their surface, with wrong-colored light (sickly purple, acid green, blood red) bleeding through. Eyes glow. Cracks glow. Readable across the isometric view at a glance. Clean and corrupted are never ambiguous.

**The Purification Pulse:**
At boss defeat, a holy/nature pulse radiates outward from the fallen boss. Corrupted sprites across the entire level switch to their clean versions — cracked grass becomes whole, soul-cracked bark closes, twisted flowers straighten. The world exhales. The transformation is immediate and complete — a visual reward that confirms the run's meaning before the warriors return home.

**Hub as Living World-State:**
- Campfire flame grows larger and brighter as the collective player level increases — a living monument to accumulated warrior strength
- Territory entrance roads dim with corruption on first approach; repeated successful runs visually clear them — the world holds the record of the warriors' effort

**Spirit Bond Tethers:**
Visible on the host screen as colored particle trails between bonded players. Each active bond uses a distinct color. Up to three tethers visible simultaneously during the boss fight.

**The Vigil:**
When warriors fall, their spirit forms linger on the battlefield — colored, luminous, surrounding the last fighter. Silent. The visual weight of up to six fallen spirits gathered around one standing warrior makes the stakes undeniable without breaking the world.

**Atmospheric Debris:**
Scattered throughout corrupted territories: remnants of old campsites, collapsed structures, debris that echoes the doodads of the hub — a dead campfire, a broken post, weathered objects. No text. No interaction. Traces of life before the corruption came, or warriors who came before. The world has history that it doesn't explain.

**Color and Lighting:**
- Hub: warm ochres, firelight orange, deep greens — safe, inhabited, alive
- Dungeon: earthy tones that dim and fracture as corruption deepens; wrong-colored light leaks through soul cracks
- Purification: clean colors return suddenly and completely at boss defeat — the contrast is the reward

---

### Audio Storytelling

**Hub Ambient:**
Warm natural sounds — wind, fire crackling, birdsong, insect chorus — layered with light tribal percussion and occasional distant chanting. Safe. Restorative. The world as it should sound.

**Dungeon Ambient:**
Natural sounds grow sparse and wrong as the level deepens. Drums become more urgent and rhythmic. Eerie undertones emerge — not horror, not dread, but urgency and strangeness. The world sounds sick.

**Combat:**
Reactive audio — percussion and discordant spirit-sound accents that escalate with enemy count and intensity. The soundscape is never static; it tracks the fight.

**Boss Encounter:**
A distinct, fully composed audio signature per boss. Not generative — a specific piece of music that belongs to this boss and no other. A moment, not a loop.

**The Purification Pulse:**
At boss defeat, a refreshing water-like sound accompanies the visual pulse — nature returning, the wound briefly closing. A sound unlike anything else in the dungeon. Clean, resonant, final.

**Spirit Bond Assignment:**
A soothing or bond-specific sound plays when the bond forms — distinct from combat audio, a moment of calm at the level-end breath.

**Spirit Forms (The Vigil):**
Silent. Their presence is visual. *(Future: abilities and auras will carry their own audio — TBD.)*

---

### Found Documents

**Approach:** No found documents, no readable text in the world.

**Atmospheric Debris:** Corrupted territories contain traces of previous life — remnants of old campsites, collapsed doodads that echo hub objects, weathered debris. These are ambient props only: no interaction, no text, no lore tooltip. Players may notice them or not. The world has been here before them.

**Rationale:** Party Delve's narrative is felt, not read. The corruption's history and the world's story are communicated through environment, VFX, and audio — not collectibles. The atmospheric debris adds depth without demanding attention.

## Narrative Delivery

### Cutscenes

**Approach:** No traditional cutscenes in v1.0. All narrative moments are in-world — camera stays fixed, gameplay continues on the host screen.

**In-world animated moments (not cutscenes):**

| Moment | What happens | Where it plays |
|---|---|---|
| **Player joins** | Player character walks into the hub through the campsite gate entrance | Host screen, in-world |
| **Class switch** | Character walks out through the gate; new class walks back in | Host screen, in-world |
| **Run end (victory)** | Warriors remain on the purified level; players move freely until they tap "Return to Camp" on controller | Host screen, free-roam |
| **Run end (failure)** | Scene ends; transition back to hub | Host screen |

**Skippable:** N/A — these are ambient in-world moments, not separate sequences.
**Interactive elements:** None.

### In-Game Storytelling

**Primary delivery methods:**

| Method | What it delivers | Interrupts gameplay? |
|---|---|---|
| **Environmental progression** | Corruption trail across levels; purification pulse at boss defeat | Never |
| **Hub world state** | Campfire growth; entrance road clearing; NPC mood shift | Never — always ambient |
| **Spirit Bond tether** | Bond formed — colored particle tether appears on host screen | Never on host |
| **Controller overlay** | Bonded players receive bond details on their own screen | Controller only; host unaffected |
| **Territory Spirit Voice** | Boss defeated / run failed — brief audio line | Non-interruptive; plays over game state |
| **In-world entry animation** | Player join and class switch conveyed through character movement | Ambient; gameplay continues |

**Show vs. Tell balance:** Almost entirely show. The world demonstrates its state; players read it. The only "tell" is the Spirit Bond controller overlay and the Territory Spirit Voice — both brief, both non-interruptive on the host.

**Interruption approach:** Zero gameplay interruptions on the host screen. The group's shared experience is never paused for story. The controller overlay is personal and parallel — it does not stop the game.

**Player control:** "Return to Camp" button on controller is the only player-driven narrative transition (end of run). Everything else is ambient and automatic.

---

### Optional Content

**Keeper dialogue:** Players can approach the Keeper between runs for enemy information and guidance — optional, never required. Depth for those who want it; invisible to those who don't.

**Class lore introductions:** Available at the class selection POI. Brief mythic text per class. Optional reading; the class is fully playable without reading it.

**Atmospheric debris:** Corrupted territory props that curious players may notice — old campsites, weathered traces of previous life. No interaction, no reward, no notification. There for those who look.

**No side quests, collectible lore, secret endings, or extended content in v1.0.**

---

### Ending Structure

**Per-run endings (two states):**

**Victory:** Boss defeated. Purification pulse sweeps the level. Territory Spirit Voice plays. Warriors remain on the now-clean level — free to roam, explore the aftermath, until each player taps "Return to Camp." No cutscene. The world is the ending.

**Failure:** All warriors enter spirit form. Territory Spirit Voice plays. Return to hub. Partial rewards applied.

**First-time milestone rewards:** Each biome boss, defeated for the first time on each difficulty tier, grants a unique reward per player — a cosmetic, a mastery marker, a recognition. Personal and repeatable across difficulties. Not a world revelation — an earned acknowledgement.

**World ending:** None. The cycle is the ending. The campfire burns a little brighter. The road is a little cleaner. The warriors return. The corruption waits.

## Gameplay Integration

### Narrative-Gameplay Connection

**Integration approach:** Party Delve has near-total ludonarrative harmony — there is no separation between "story sections" and "gameplay sections." The narrative is expressed entirely through mechanics, environment, and ambient audio. Players are not told the story; they enact it.

**Mechanic-Theme Alignment:**

| Theme | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| **Bonds Forged in Struggle** | Spirit Bond system — warriors are literally bonded through shared peril; bonds accumulate run to run and are visible on the host screen as tethers |
| **Healing a Wounded World** | The goal is purification, not destruction — players cleanse corrupted spirits rather than simply killing enemies; the purification pulse visualises the healing |
| **Knowledge Through Adversity** | Corruption readability deepens across levels; mastery system rewards repeated engagement with a class; veterans read enemy patterns newcomers cannot yet see |
| **The Covenant is Ongoing** | The roguelike structure IS the narrative — no authored ending, only cycles; returning to the hub and going out again is the story |

**Story-gameplay balance:** 100% integrated. There are no dedicated "story sections" that pause gameplay. Every narrative beat — the purification pulse, the Territory Spirit Voice, the Spirit Bond tether, the hub's brightening — plays within or immediately adjacent to the gameplay flow.

**Ludonarrative harmony:** Exceptional. The game asks players to purify a wounded world together — and the mechanics ARE collective purification. There is no tension between what the game says and what the game asks players to do.

---

### Story Gating

**Gating approach:** One meaningful progression gate; everything else is freely accessible.

**Story-Locked Elements:**

| Gate | Requirement | Narrative framing |
|---|---|---|
| **Ancient Forest road** | Defeat the Grassland biome boss | The Forest corruption runs deeper and older — warriors prove themselves on the Plains before the Forest road opens |

**No other story gates.** Hub NPCs, Keeper dialogue, class lore introductions, and the Vendor are accessible from first arrival.

**Mandatory narrative beats** (auto-play, no player trigger required):
- Territory Spirit Voice at boss defeat
- Territory Spirit Voice at run failure
- Spirit Bond tether and controller overlay at level end
- Purification pulse at boss defeat

**Optional narrative content:**
- Keeper dialogue (tutorials, enemy knowledge)
- Class lore introductions at class selection POI
- Atmospheric debris in corrupted territories

---

### Player Agency

**Agency level:** Flavor choices within a fixed world.

The world's story is fixed — the corruption exists, the cycle continues, the covenant holds. Players do not choose the outcome of the world. What players choose is how they engage with it.

**What players control:**
- Which biome to run (within unlock order)
- Which class to play — which tribe's tradition to carry
- Which difficulty to attempt
- When to advance after each Spirit Bond moment (the only player-paced narrative beat)
- When to return to camp after a victory

**What players do not control:**
- Spirit Bond assignments (random, derived from the run seed)
- The corruption's nature or the cycle's continuation
- Story outcomes — there is no authored divergence

**Role-playing freedom:** High within the framework. The warrior archetype is the tribe; the personality is the player's. No authored dialogue choices, no moral decisions — the player IS the warrior, and the warrior's voice is their own.

**Dynamic narrative:** The hub world state — campfire brightness, territory entrance clarity, NPC mood — responds to cumulative player actions across sessions. The world remembers, even if it doesn't editorialize.
